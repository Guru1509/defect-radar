### Pattern read

- **Forward collision avoidance:** The run begins in May 2021 with counts around 87–114 in its first six months (shares 2.04–3.07%), after a higher and variable pre-run year. A very large one-month spike follows in February 2022 (1,059; 21.494%), then counts settle mostly in the low hundreds and shares around 5–9%. This is a sustained category-level elevation with a sharp transient peak, not a single isolated onset. During the run, the historical count upper bound's median rises only from about 22 to 40; after it, the same-month history has adapted substantially. Counts remain elevated after flags stop (post-run median 309 vs. pre-run 68), while the post-run median upper bounds are about 591 counts and 15.082% share.
- **Back-over prevention:** Counts rise from a pre-run median of 35 to 53 in the first run year and 85.5 in the second; post-run median is still 75. Shares likewise rise from 0.847% to 1.214% and 1.742%, then remain at 1.371%. This looks more like a gradual increase/step-up that persists than a one-month spike. The baseline later catches up enough that none of the following 12 months flags.
- **Lane departure:** The 24-month run has medians of 67 and 80 counts (1.440% and 1.561% share) across its two years, compared with 50 (1.214%) in the preceding year. Counts/shares remain higher after the run, but only one of the next 12 months flags; the growing historical reference absorbs much of the sustained level.
- **Engine and engine cooling:** The 16-month run is part of a broader, ongoing pattern: four of the preceding 12 months were already flagged, and nine of the following 12 months are flagged. Post-run medians rise to 159 counts and 2.845% share versus pre-run 41 and 1.130%; the saved historical upper-bound medians remain 64.926 counts and 1.262% share. This is continuing elevation, not a cleanly bounded episode in the available series.

Counts alone do not identify why any category changed. In particular, the output contains no vehicle-exposure denominator, and the broad component labels can aggregate different complaints. These patterns do not establish a defect, cause, investigation timing, or recall relationship.

## Same-calendar baseline limitation

The detector compares each month only with up to five earlier observations of that same calendar month. During a sustained increase, those comparisons can remain mostly pre-shift, so the same established elevation is flagged repeatedly—as in the 30-month forward-collision run and the 24-month back-over/lane-departure runs. As shifted values enter the rolling history, its median and MAD/upper bound can rise until an elevated current level is no longer flagged. Forward collision avoidance illustrates this: flags cease after October 2023 even though subsequent counts and shares remain well above the pre-run level. Conversely, engine/engine cooling remains frequently flagged after its first run because its saved same-month thresholds are still much lower. The current output treats each month as a separate flag; it has no state for “new onset” versus “already established shift.”

## Recommended testable method (not implemented)

Add an **episode state machine** as a separate analysis layer, leaving the existing detector unchanged:

1. A first eligible month exceeding both existing bounds opens a *candidate* episode.
2. Confirm one episode onset at the first month if three consecutive eligible months exceed both bounds; isolated one-month spikes do not confirm an episode.
3. On confirmation, freeze the pre-onset same-calendar-month upper bounds as the episode reference. Label subsequent elevated months *established shift*, not new increases. Close the episode only after three consecutive eligible months fall below both frozen bounds; then re-arm for a later onset.

Test on synthetic series containing a one-month spike, a sustained step, a gradual ramp, and a return to baseline. Expected results: no confirmed episode for the isolated spike; one onset for the sustained step/ramp, with later months classified as persistence; and closure only after the sustained return. This is a proposal for distinguishing onset from persistence, not a validated defect detector or recall predictor.
